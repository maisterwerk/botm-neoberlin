# Domain 2, round 2 — RED-TEAM REVIEW (verbatim)

**On the Contamination Expert.** The taxonomy is fine; the ranking is wrong. It calls mechanism 2 (paraphrase leakage) "cheapest to hide," but the entire lab's detection stack rests on paraphrase-invariance. If a lab trains on paraphrased test items — or simply does 3 epochs of instruction-tuning on GSM8K-style regenerations — arms B and C become *the training distribution*, not the control. Δ = acc(A) − acc(B/C/D) goes to zero and the audit certifies a contaminated model as clean. This is not a corner case: synthetic-data pipelines produce exactly this by default. Mechanism 5 has the same hole reversed — error-pattern agreement on mislabelled gold assumes idiosyncratic errors; two models trained on the same web corpus share error patterns *without* distillation. Base rate of shared-corpus agreement is unmeasured, so the "far above chance" threshold is undefined.

**On the Statistician.** The DiD is not identified. It requires that O and F be exchangeable under no contamination — but F is authored post-cutoff by commissioned writers, and 2PL IRT anchored on a *reference-model panel* equates difficulty for the reference panel, not for M_new. If M_new has genuinely improved on the ability dimension the anchors don't span (say, long-horizon arithmetic), that improvement shows up as p_new,O − p_new,F > 0 and is scored as contamination. The protocol cannot distinguish "memorised O" from "new capability that fresh items happen not to test." That is the entire research question, and the design assumes it away.

Power numbers: n ≥ (2.80/Δ)² with Δ=0.10 → 784 is a proportions-scale approximation with σ² fixed at 0.25; near p=0.85 the true variance is 0.1275, so 784 overstates need — but the *four-way* DiD variance is roughly 4× a single arm, so ~1530 is if anything too small, not conservative. And the Designer commissions **300** fresh items. The two specialists are off by a factor of five and nobody noticed.

**On the Experiment Designer.** The 5-point flag threshold is asserted, never derived, and is uncalibrated against the 12-point headline gain. The F-acceptance rule ("reference accuracy on F within 2 points of A") *selects fresh items to match original difficulty for old models* — this actively filters out exactly the novel items that would reveal genuine new capability. It manufactures the null.

**Falsification is circular.** Injecting k% of O by fine-tuning creates verbatim memorisation — mechanism 1, the one the Expert says is cheapest to defeat. The protocol validates itself only against the threat model it already handles. No injection test covers paraphrase-trained or distillation contamination.

**Most damaging flaw:** the protocol's null and alternative are not separable — every statistic scores "does better on old items than new" as contamination, but a genuine capability gain concentrated on skills the fresh items underweight produces an identical signature, and the difficulty-equating step is *designed* to remove the items that could tell them apart.
